neutrino mass readout inside the \emph{Void and Form} invariant ledger.  A

\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Oscillation experiments measure neutrino mass splittings, but the absolute
mass scale remains outside the Standard Model.  A serious prediction of that
scale must do more than write down small numbers.  It must specify the finite
candidate space, show which candidates are eliminated, state every external
physical input, and separate the checked arithmetic from the empirical claim
that the resulting chain describes the world.

The construction below follows that discipline.  The first formal output is
the neutral-depth square $5^2=25$.  The electron-anchored reading is retained
as a low-energy comparison layer.  The Planck-anchored layer then classifies
the route to the electron scale, the suppression exponents, the running
factor, the correction tower action, the charged-sector correction data, and
the normal-hierarchy observables.  None of those choices is selected by
decimal agreement with measured neutrino masses; the decimals are read only
after the rational chain has been fixed.

The charged and neutral residual layers are treated differently.  The charged
mass ratios still carry structured ppm-level residuals whose sign pattern
belongs to the physical comparison with hadronic and leptonic sectors.  The
neutral splitting residual is classified at the carrier level.  Matching
weights are blocked by $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$-uniformity, so the residual acts
one layer above the matchings.  The surviving carrier is the coupling
complement $q-p$ over $q$, with $q=306$, $p=11$, and $q-p=295$.

\section*{Scientific claim and validation}

The formal claim of this paper is narrow: within the displayed finite
candidate spaces, the rational chain from neutral depth to final Planck
ratios is mechanically checked in Agda under \texttt{--safe} and
\texttt{--without-K}.  The physical claim is stronger and remains exposed:
the exact rational readout is proposed as the normal neutrino mass hierarchy
of nature.

For the paper to count as a scientific contribution rather than a formal
curiosity, six obligations must remain visible.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The Agda file must type-check from a clean checkout, without postulates
  or unsafe flags.
\item The candidate spaces must be explicit enough that the survivor is not a
  fitted choice hidden behind notation.
\item The Planck input, unit conventions, and low-energy comparison constants
  must be named rather than absorbed into the derivation.
\item The exact rational outputs must be stated before the electronvolt

\item The comparison with oscillation data, cosmological mass bounds, beta

  nonzero lightest mass beyond the stated boundary, a conflicting mass sum, or

  identification, not the formal arithmetic.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Structure of the paper}

The large programme is a dependency chain.  It starts at the boundary
argument for distinction, crosses the formal threshold only after the
binary datum has been supplied, and ends only when a dimensionful mass
number can be compared with experiment.  Every link must preserve the
status of the previous link.

The intended route is:
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\mathrm{distinction}
\to \mathrm{binary\ normal\ form}
\to K_4\,\mathrm{closure}
\to \mathrm{invariant\ ledger} \\
\to \mathrm{number\ tower}

\to \mathrm{mass\ ratios}
\to \mathrm{correction/running}
\to \mathrm{absolute\ scale}
\to \mathrm{particle\ masses}.
\end{array}
\]

The paper follows that route in the following order.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Use the distinction-to-$K_4$ kernel supplied by \emph{Void} without
  reopening its proof.
\item Import only the small invariant ledger needed for the neutral
  problem: $V,E,d,\chi,\kappa$, the Fermat slots, loop numerator, and
  loop volumes.
\item Classify neutral-depth candidates and prove the finite survivor
  statement for depth $5$ and denominator $25$.
\item Record the electron-anchored neutrino prediction as a physical
  comparison candidate, because the charged-mass ledger is expressed in
  ratios to $m_e$.
\item Classify the Planck-to-low-energy bridge.  The bridge must explain
  the large suppression from the Planck unit to the electron and neutral
  scale without inserting a tunable parameter.
\item State the resulting Planck-ratio particle masses and the neutrino
  observables that follow from them.
\item Compare the resulting masses with experiment, including charged
  leptons, quarks where the scheme applies, neutrino splittings, total
  neutrino mass, beta-decay bounds, and neutrinoless-double-beta limits.
  This comparison is not an Agda theorem; it is the empirical boundary.
\end{enumerate}

not moved beyond the charged generation count.  The closure slot fails
because it is the already-used four-case carrier.  The edge slot fails
because it belongs to the full edge sector.  Only the neutral depth
survives all three filters.

\section{Planck-to-low-energy bridge classification}

The bridge problem can now be stated without drama.  The Planck anchor
is available as the natural ultraviolet unit, and the neutral denominator
is available from the finite schema.  The first predicate records why the
uncorrected routes do not suffice: direct Planck division stays in the
ultraviolet sector; the Higgs route names an intermediate scale without
deriving the running; the bare electron route carries only the structured
suppression denominator; and the electron-only route uses a low-energy
physical anchor.

The corrected predicate then adds the missing structure.  It requires
the Planck-suppression survivor, the running survivor, the complete
correction-tower interface, the canonical tower action, and the absence
of an empirical patch.  In this finite candidate space, only the
sign-ordered tower reading survives.  This is the formal bridge used in
the mass-chain section below.

\section{Exact electron-anchored prediction candidate}

The Planck scale is the natural scale for the ultraviolet cutoff: in the
discrete reading, one lattice edge may be read as one Planck length and
the corresponding cutoff energy as one Planck mass.  This fixes the unit
in which a low-energy particle mass is measured; it does not select such
a mass by itself.  If the denominator below were applied directly to the
Planck mass, the result would be a Planck-sector
excitation, not a neutrino-scale mass: $M_P/(4\cdot137^3) \approx
1.19\cdot10^{21}\,\mathrm{eV}$.  To obtain a $0.05\,\mathrm{eV}$
neutrino directly from $M_P$, the ledger would need an additional
suppression of about $2.46\cdot10^{29}$.  The Planck-to-low-energy
bridge supplying that suppression is classified in the Planck-anchored
layer; the electron-anchored layer is retained as a comparison reading.

The exact prediction needs one physical scale.  The choice made here is
the electron mass, because the mass ledger developed here expresses
charged-particle masses as ratios to $m_e$.  The neutral prediction is
therefore not an electron-volt theorem.  It is an exact dimensionless
claim:
\[
  m_1 = 0, \qquad
  \frac{m_2}{m_e} = \frac{1}{6\cdot 4\cdot 137^3}, \qquad
  \frac{m_3}{m_e} = \frac{1}{4\cdot 137^3}.
\]
Here $137$ is the closure integer, the exponent $3$ is the generation
depth, $4$ is the $K_4$ carrier, and $6$ is the edge-sector separation
between the two nonzero neutral masses.  The neutral-depth interface
above remains present: it locks the admissible neutral slot at depth $5$
with square $25$.  The scale formula below is the additional physical
reading that turns the slot into an absolute mass proposal.

Using $m_e = 510998.950\,\mathrm{eV}$ only as the external scale, this
candidate gives
\[
  m_1 = 0, \qquad
  m_2 = 0.008280318928\,\mathrm{eV}, \qquad
  m_3 = 0.049681913568\,\mathrm{eV},
\]
and
\[
  \sum_i m_i = 0.057962232497\,\mathrm{eV}.
\]
Those decimals are not hard-coded below; the checked content is the
integer denominator pattern and the boundary level of the scale anchor.

\section{Planck-anchored mass-chain}

The Planck-anchored chain begins with the survivor of a finite route
classification and three finite slot classifications.  The route is
forced to the Planck-to-electron bridge; the $\kappa$ exponent is forced
to the Euler slot; the Fermat exponent is forced to the carrier slot; and
the closure exponent is forced to the carrier-plus-generation slot.
Together with the already proved neutral survivor this gives the
canonical denominator
\[
  5\cdot 8^2\cdot 17^4\cdot 137^7
\]
without using the decimal comparison as a filter.

Read by itself, this bare survivor already gives exact rational
multiples of $M_P$.  With $M_P \approx
1.22089\cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{eV}$ it yields, before the running and
tower corrections, approximately

  m_e = 504297.6015\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
  m_p = 925.8904\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad

  m_\tau = 1774.6233\,\mathrm{MeV},

and

  m_2 = 0.0081717291\,\mathrm{eV},\quad

\]
These numbers are consequences of the classified survivor.  They are not
the reason it was selected.  They are also not the final reading of the
chain: the bare bridge lacks the running and tower action that the next
filters require.

The running gate is also classified rather than fitted.  The survivor is
the degree-weighted RG loop correction

  m_2 = 0.0082810831\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
  \sum_i m_i = 0.0579675818\,\mathrm{eV}.
\]
These corrected numbers are again consequences, not filters.  The
running-only stage is intermediate: the full correction tower follows.

The correction tower has a finite interface: two positive terms
\[
  \frac{2}{137^2},\quad \frac{1}{8\cdot 3\cdot 137^2}
\]
and two negative terms
\[
  -\frac{3^2}{8\cdot 137^2},\quad
  -\frac{(11+2)^2}{8^2\cdot 137^2}.

first, then the two negative terms.  This gives the exact tower factor
\[
  \frac{1524775277821401191187}{1524915363442855784448}.
\]
Composed with the running-corrected Planck chain, this yields

  m_e = 510999.1626\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
  m_p = 938.1945\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad
  m_\mu = 105.7768\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad
  m_\tau = 1798.2061\,\mathrm{MeV},
\]
and
\[
  m_3 = 0.0496819342\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
  m_2 = 0.0082803224\,\mathrm{eV},\quad
  \sum_i m_i = 0.0579622566\,\mathrm{eV}.
\]

squared Planck ratios before the neutral residual carrier is applied.  In
eV units, using the same Planck input, they read approximately

  \Delta m^2_{21} = 6.8564\cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV}^2,
  \qquad
  \Delta m^2_{31} = 2.4683\cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV}^2.
\]

\section{Residual layer: forced kind and carrier boundary}

The first-order corrections classified above leave a structured residual
when the rational ratios are read against precision data.  Two
observations are taken as input here, not as choices.  In the charged
sector the ppm-level residual carries a sign asymmetry: the hadronic
ratio $p/e$ misses on the negative side, while both leptonic ratios
$\mu/e$ and $\tau/e$ miss on the positive side.  In the neutral sector
both squared-mass differences miss on the positive side, but their
relative shifts are not equal: a single multiplicative factor on
$m_\nu$ would preserve $\Delta m^2_{31}/\Delta m^2_{21}$, and that ratio
is not preserved.

The residual layer is therefore classified at the level of \emph{kind}.
Three candidate kinds are admitted formally and one is forced by the
data.  In the charged sector, a global Planck-scale shift and a uniform
running factor would impose a single sign on all charged residuals; both
are eliminated by the observed Proton/Lepton sign split.  Only a
\emph{sector-asymmetric} residual kind survives.  In the neutral sector,
a single multiplicative shift on the neutrino mass scale is eliminated
by the non-unit splitting ratio; only a \emph{splitting-asymmetric}
residual kind survives.

The kind classification is the first formal output of this layer.  In
the charged sector it records a physical comparison boundary: the sign
split is visible, but no exact charged residual carrier is selected by
decimal matching.  In the neutral sector the carrier is classified below
by the coupling complement.  Thus the charged residual is
kept as \texttt{physical-test}, while the neutral residual is carried to
a formal ratio.

\section{Required-input classification for the carrier}

The kind classification fixes \emph{what} the residual layer must do.
It does not fix \emph{which} ledger slot carries it.  Rather than
guessing a slot by decimal matching, we classify the minimal structural
input that any carrier candidate must provide before it can close.  The
candidate space is intentionally small and exhaustive at the level of
admissible inputs.

The data fix two structural axes for the charged sector: a hadronic
versus leptonic sign split, and a $\mu$ versus $\tau$ magnitude split.
For the neutral sector the data fix one axis: a $\Delta m^2_{21}$
versus $\Delta m^2_{31}$ magnitude split.  An admissible input must
provide the corresponding resolution.  The empirical patch input is
globally excluded by the bridge classification above.

Two clean structural facts follow.  The charged carrier requires both
\texttt{sign-resolution} and \texttt{generation-resolution}; a
higher-order-tower input alone does not produce a sign split, and a
sign-resolution input alone does not separate $\mu$ from $\tau$.  The
neutral carrier requires \texttt{generation-resolution}; a uniform
neutral shift cannot reproduce the non-unit splitting ratio.  The
\texttt{higher-order-tower} input remains admissible only as a
combination partner, never as a sole carrier.  The
\texttt{empirical-patch} input is forbidden on principle.

The basic $K_4$ ledger used up to this point does not carry an internal
hadronic/leptonic $\mathbf{Z}_2$ distinction.  The carrier closure therefore
requires a genuine ledger extension, not a recycling of occupied slots.  The
next section supplies that extension from the
intrinsic bipartitions and perfect matchings of $K_4$.

\section{Internal ledger extension: bipartition and matching}

The previous section recorded that the carrier closure requires a
ledger extension.  The next finite question is: which extension is
admissible without importing structure foreign to $K_4$?  Two intrinsic
$K_4$ refinements are available before any external object is
considered.  First, the four vertices admit a bipartition into two
pairs; the six edges then split into four cross edges and two internal
edges.  This is a $\mathbf{Z}_2$ refinement of the vertex set, and it
is intrinsic to $K_4$.  Second, $K_4$ admits three perfect matchings,
each consisting of two disjoint edges; this is an intrinsic three-fold
refinement of the edge set.  Together they provide a sign resolution
and a generation resolution drawn entirely from $K_4$ itself.

The candidate space below is exhaustive at the level of where the
extension is taken from.  An empirical flag is forbidden by the
discipline established above.  A foreign group adds a structure that is
not visible in $K_4$ and would be a free choice.  The pure bipartition
provides a sign resolution but no generation refinement.  The pure
matching set provides a generation refinement but no sign resolution.
Only the joint intrinsic extension provides both, and it does so
without importing anything outside $K_4$.

The intrinsic extension carries two integer counts that are forced by
$K_4$ alone: the number of vertex bipartitions and the number of
perfect matchings.  A bipartition of four vertices into two unordered
pairs is determined by choosing two vertices for one side and dividing
out the swap, giving three.  Each perfect matching of $K_4$ is in
bijection with such a bipartition by choosing the two cross edges that
share no vertex, giving three again.  Both counts equal three, and
this coincidence is the formal source of the three-fold refinement
used by the carrier.

\section{Cell structure forced by the intrinsic extension}

The intrinsic extension produces a finite cell table.  Each cell is a
pair of a bipartition side and a matching index.  Two sides times three
matchings gives six cells, and the six edges of $K_4$ provide the
counting witness: each edge belongs to exactly one matching and joins
exactly one ordered pair of bipartition sides.  The cell count and the
edge count therefore coincide as a structural identity, not as a
numerical fit.

The cell table has a forced internal structure: each side carries
exactly three cells, indexed by the three matchings.  In the leptonic
sector this matches the three observed charged leptons.  In the
hadronic sector the structure forces three generation slots, of which
the proton observable occupies one.  The remaining two hadronic slots
are predictions of the cell table, not data.  They are recorded here as
formal slots whose physical content is a separate identification.

\begin{code}

  side-B : BipartitionSide

data MatchingIndex : Set where
  matching-one   : MatchingIndex
  matching-two   : MatchingIndex
  matching-three : MatchingIndex

cell-count : ℕ
cell-count = 2 * 3

cell-count-is-six = refl

  cell-count ≡ edges canonicalK4Ledger
cell-count-equals-edges = refl

  generation-slots-per-side ≡ 3
generation-slots-per-side-is-three = refl
\end{code}

The next predicate names the observed cells.  By the discipline
established above, the side and matching assignment is a physical
reading.  Naming it here is therefore a record of \emph{which physical
content is consistent with the cell structure}, not a derivation.  The
proton occupies one hadronic slot; the electron, muon and tau occupy
the three leptonic slots.  The two unfilled hadronic slots are
predictions of the cell structure that the first-order ledger leaves to
physical identification against measured masses.

\section{Automorphism invariance forbids matching-differential weights}

The intrinsic carrier closure forces three perfect matchings on $K_4$
and assigns the three observed leptonic generations to those three
matchings.  In principle one could try to absorb the residual ratio
gap between $m_3/m_2 = 6$ and the experimental value $\sqrt{33.55}
\approx 5.79$ by giving each matching its own multiplicative weight.
The block below classifies that move and rules it out at the formal
level.  $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4) = S_4$ acts transitively on the three
perfect matchings; any K\textsubscript{4}-intrinsic weight assignment
that depends on the matching index breaks $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$
invariance.  Under the joint conditions
\emph{$K_4$-intrinsic, $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$-invariant, no external
generator}, the only admissible weight pattern is uniform across all
three matchings.  Hence no purely intrinsic mechanism can produce a
matching-differential factor on the neutral pair.

\section{Aut\texorpdfstring{$(K_4)$}{(K4)}-invariance on the matching
weights as a real classification theorem}

The previous block stated the consequence of automorphism invariance
on a finite list of named patterns by a table.  The block below proves
the underlying classification on the open function space of all
matching weights.  A weight is any function from the three matching
indices into $\mathbb{N}$.  The automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)
= S_4$ acts on the four vertices of $K_4$ and induces an action on the
three perfect matchings via the surjection $S_4 \twoheadrightarrow
S_3$; under this induced action the three matchings form a single
orbit.  We use the two adjacent transpositions
$(\text{matching-one}\ \text{matching-two})$ and
$(\text{matching-two}\ \text{matching-three})$ as generators; both
descend from $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$ because each corresponds to a vertex
permutation that fixes one matching and swaps the other two.  Their
fixed points are the constant weights.

\begin{code}

\section{Vertex permutations from
\texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$}{Aut(K4)} as the source of the two
matching generators}

The previous block proved the classification on
\texttt{MatchingPermutation}, an abstract two-element generator
family.  The block below grounds that family in
$\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$.  We introduce the four vertices of $K_4$, the
six edges, the membership of each edge in a perfect matching, two
specific vertex transpositions
$(\mathrm{v_1}\ \mathrm{v_2})$ and $(\mathrm{v_2}\ \mathrm{v_3})$
together with proofs that they are involutions and hence bijections,
and the generic induced action of an arbitrary vertex permutation on
matchings via its edge image.  We then check by case analysis that
the induced action of $(\mathrm{v_1}\ \mathrm{v_2})$ coincides with
the abstract generator \texttt{swap-two-three} and that the induced
action of $(\mathrm{v_2}\ \mathrm{v_3})$ coincides with
\texttt{swap-one-two}.  Composing with the previous classification
theorem yields the master statement: every weight on the three
matchings that is invariant under the two named vertex transpositions
is constant.  Since $K_4$ is the complete graph on four vertices,
every vertex bijection is an automorphism of $K_4$, so the two
specific transpositions are genuine elements of $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$.

\section{Neutral residual carrier classification}

The matching theorem closes the tempting but wrong local move.  A
neutral residual that changes the splitting ratio cannot be carried by
matching weights, because any $K_4$-intrinsic matching weight that
respects the vertex automorphisms is uniform across the three perfect
matchings.  The residual carrier must therefore act one layer above the
matching index.

The finite candidate space has exactly three entries.  There may be no
residual carrier; the residual may be placed directly on the matchings;
or the residual may be carried by the already available coupling
complement.  The four filters are the closure contract of this section:
the carrier must be splitting-asymmetric, must preserve the
$\mathrm{Aut}(K_4)$-uniformity of matching weights, must use a ledger
complement already present, and must introduce no empirical patch.  Under
those filters only the coupling-complement carrier survives.  This is the
formal closure of the carrier slot; the physical reading as a neutrino
splitting correction remains an empirical test.

\section{Spectral signature of \texorpdfstring{$K_4$}{K4}: a structural source for $1+3$}

The adjacency operator of $K_4$ has the form $A = J - I$, where $J$ is
the all-ones matrix on four vertices.  Its spectrum is fixed by graph
data alone: the trace vanishes (no self-loops), the trace of $A^2$
equals $2|E| = 12$ (twice the edge count), and the Perron eigenvalue
equals the regular degree $d=3$.  These three integer constraints
force the multiset $\{+3,\,-1,\,-1,\,-1\}$ as the unique spectrum.
The combinatorial Laplacian $L = dI - A$ then has the spectrum
$\{0,\,4,\,4,\,4\}$: one zero mode (the constant function on the four
vertices) and three degenerate positive modes at the value $V=4$.

Both spectra split the four modes as $1+3$ with opposite signs.  In
the adjacency picture: one positive Perron mode against three
degenerate negative modes.  In the Laplacian picture: one zero
(non-propagating) mode against three degenerate positive modes.  This
$1+3$ split is a theorem of $K_4$, not a choice.

The split is the structural source of every $1+3$ asymmetry that
appears in the physical reading: the time-versus-space split of a
$(-,+,+,+)$ Lorentz signature, the distinguished-versus-degenerate
character of a Perron mode, and the asymmetric branch of the charged
residual classification.  The identification of the distinguished
spectral mode with the physical name ``time'' or ``hadronic sector'',
and of the three degenerate modes with ``space'' or ``leptonic
generations'', is a physical reading and is recorded as
\texttt{physical-test}.  What is closed at the formal level is only
the existence and the cardinality of the split.

\begin{code}
-- $ SpectralEntry.
data SpectralEntry : Set where
  positive-mode : ℕ → SpectralEntry
